A pegboard on a desk frame that was never meant to have one.

Four printed brackets hook into the slotted uprights of an Ergotron LAN Organizer 3000 and carry an IKEA SKÅDIS. No drilling, no wall, no rail — and a captured square nut inside each bracket gives you 12 mm to slide the board into place before you tighten anything.

The joint

A square nut, sealed inside the plate

An M4 square nut, measured 7 × 7 × 3 mm, slides in from the plate's outboard edge while the bracket is still in your hand, and after that it is captive: the face the board lands on is solid but for the screw slot.

  1. Upright face metal. The plate's back bears flat on it; the hooks wrap through the punched slots.
  2. The plate, 11.0 mm. Not chosen — derived from the screw length.
  3. Nut cavity, 7.4 × 3.4 mm. Open at one edge only. 7.4 is under the nut's 9.9 mm diagonal, so it cannot turn.
  4. The nut. Slides the length of the cavity; that travel is the adjustment.
  5. The 4 mm wall. Tightening pulls the nut forward onto this, which makes it an ordinary clamped bolt.
  6. Screw slot, 16.8 mm long. One cut through the full thickness, so the tip passes behind the nut.
  7. SKÅDIS board, 6 mm. Clamped flat between the screw head and the plate.
  8. The IKEA decorative M4 screw, 15 mm. Its tip stops 2 mm short of the plate's back face.

Why not just bolt through? Because a bolt through a fixed hole has no adjustment, and neither did the two designs before this one. The board's slots repeat every 40 mm and the desk's uprights are 735.04 mm apart; those two numbers do not agree, and the difference has to go somewhere. Here it goes into a nut that slides.

The part

Measured, not assumed

Every guess in this project was wrong

And every measurement has held. Two measured numbers set the whole part.

6.0 mm

The board. Not the 4.6 mm both community SKÅDIS libraries quote. Everything downstream of a screw length depends on it.

15 mm

The decorative screw. Through a 6 mm board that leaves 9 mm behind it with nowhere to go — which is what sets the plate.

plateThickness = screwLength - boardThickness + screwTipClearance
               = 15.0 - 6.0 + 2.0
               = 11.0 mm

Change the screw and the plate follows. The build refuses outright if the tip would come within 0.5 mm of the back face, or if it would bottom out inside the cavity instead of passing behind the nut.

Mounting

Two grids that do not agree

One cavity per bracket, four brackets per board, two per upright. That puts vertical alignment in the assembly — where the upright's 25.4 mm pitch has to meet the board's 40 mm one. A screw has 11 mm of freedom inside its slot, and that is the entire budget.

Wider is better. The two brackets on one upright are what stop the board rotating, and the moment arm is the distance between them — so take the widest spacing the board has room for, not the first one that works.

The build script prints that list on every run, so it cannot get lost in a README nobody rereads. Left and right brackets are mirrored, each putting its cavity 7.52 mm toward the middle of the desk — where the board's nearest slot column falls at the measured spacing.

Printing

One orientation matters more than the rest

Lay the bracket on a side face so the hook profile is drawn inside every layer instead of stacked across them. The cavity and the screw slot then need no support at all — they run along the build direction — and supports grow only under the two hooks.

Put the inboard edge on the bed. --rotate-x -90 for the left bracket, --rotate-x 90 for the right. Mouth-down is the intuitive choice and it fails quietly: it puts the cavity's blind end up top, where its ceiling is a horizontal overhang, and the slicer fills the whole cavity with support you can never get out. Sliced both ways, supports reach z 29.0 mouth-down against z 21.6 mouth-up.

PETG

Sufficient, and arguably better than ASA here: the 2.4 mm hook tab is tougher in PETG, and nothing warps. Stresses run 2–5% of yield.

4 walls · 40%

Stock “structural” profiles ship thinner than this, and the hooks carry the whole board.

0.25 mm

Support contact distance. PETG welds itself to organic supports at the usual 0.2.

The supports touch the side face of the hook tabs — the face that enters the slot. Put a caliper on a tab after cleanup: nominal 2.4 mm, and it has to stay under about 2.6 mm to enter a 3.2 mm slot.

STL, STEP, F3D and the scripts that make them

Everything is parametric in Fusion 360, driven through its local MCP server. Every user parameter drives geometry, with lengths in millimetres, angles in degrees and counts unitless — the build fails if that stops being true.

Where this started

This project started from Skadis to Kallax mount adapter by WegBier, licensed CC BY-NC-SA, which is where the idea of mounting to SKÅDIS with a printed part rather than bolting through it came from. That licence is incompatible with this repository's MIT licence, so none of that model's geometry has ever been reproduced here, and this design shares no feature with it.

SKÅDIS interface dimensions are measurements of IKEA's product rather than anyone's authorship, cross-checked against franpoli/OpenSCADutil and TassSinclair/skadis, then re-measured on the board itself.
